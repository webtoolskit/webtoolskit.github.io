What is this Tool?

The Study Planner helps students create a balanced, subject-wise study schedule based on the number of days until their exam, daily study hours, and the relative importance or difficulty of each subject. Effective study planning is one of the most underrated academic skills — students who plan their preparation consistently outperform those who study randomly, even with the same total hours invested.

How to Use This Tool

2

5

Why This Matters

  • Eliminates the panic of unplanned last-minute cramming
  • Ensures balanced coverage across all subjects
  • Allocates more time to difficult or high-weightage subjects
  • Builds in rest days to prevent burnout
  • Creates a clear daily target to track progress against

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many hours should I study per day for exams?
A: 6-8 hours of focused study per day is optimal for most students during exam preparation. Beyond 8-9 hours, diminishing returns set in due to mental fatigue. Quality matters more than quantity.
Q: Should I study one subject all day or switch between subjects?
A: Interleaving — switching between subjects during a study session — leads to better long-term retention than blocking. Study one subject for 90-120 minutes, then switch. Review what you studied the previous day at the start of each session.
Q: How far in advance should I start exam preparation?
A: For semester exams: 4-6 weeks. For competitive exams like JEE/NEET: 1-2 years. For board exams: 3-4 months. Starting early allows multiple revision cycles, which is the biggest predictor of exam success.
Q: What is the Pomodoro technique for studying?
A: Study for 25 minutes with full focus, then take a 5-minute break. After 4 such cycles, take a 20-30 minute break. This technique maintains concentration and prevents mental fatigue during long study sessions.
💡 Pro Tip: The most effective study strategy is active recall — testing yourself on what you've studied rather than re-reading. Use flashcards, practice papers, or simply close the book and write down everything you remember after each study session.
