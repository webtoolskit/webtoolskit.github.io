What is this Tool?

The Client Lifetime Value (CLV) Calculator helps freelancers understand the true long-term worth of a client relationship — not just a single project fee. When you know a client's lifetime value, you can make better decisions about how much time to invest in acquisition, how aggressively to price retention, and which clients deserve premium service and attention. A client who gives you ₹30,000 per project four times a year for three years — and refers two more clients — is worth far more than their first invoice suggests.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good client lifetime value?
This varies hugely by industry and rates. The key insight is that LTV is almost always much higher than most freelancers realise — which is why client retention is so much more valuable than constantly chasing new clients.
How do I increase client lifetime value?
Deliver excellent work consistently, communicate proactively, offer new services as their business grows, send referral incentives, and check in periodically even when there's no active project.
Is it worth offering discounts for long-term clients?
A 10-15% discount for a client who commits to 6+ months of work can be worth it if it increases their lifetime value. However, avoid discounts with clients who never commit to long-term work.
How do I get more referrals?
Simply ask. Most satisfied clients are happy to refer but don't think to do it proactively. After delivering great work, ask: 'Do you know anyone else who might benefit from this kind of help?'
What is client lifetime value for freelancers?
Client lifetime value (LTV) is the total revenue a client is likely to bring you over the whole relationship, including repeat projects and referrals. It helps you decide which clients deserve priority, discounts or extra effort.
How do I calculate client lifetime value?
Client LTV = average project value × projects per year × years you expect to work together, plus referral value. For example, ₹25,000 projects, 4 a year, for 3 years is ₹3,00,000 — add one ₹25,000 referral a year and it becomes ₹3,75,000.
Which clients should I focus on as a freelancer?
Prioritise clients with high lifetime value, fair rates and low stress. A client who sends steady ₹25,000 projects every quarter is usually worth more than a one-off ₹60,000 project that comes with endless revisions.
When should I let go of a client?
Consider it when a client's lifetime value is low and they take disproportionate time — late payments, scope creep, constant urgent requests. Freeing up that time lets you take on better-paying work.
How do I get more repeat clients?
Deliver on time, communicate clearly and suggest the next project before the current one ends. A short check-in after delivery and a retainer offer for ongoing work turn one-off clients into repeat ones.
What is a retainer client?
A retainer client pays you a fixed monthly fee for an agreed amount of work or availability. Retainers make your income predictable and usually have the highest lifetime value of any client type.
How much is a referral worth?
Enter how many referrals a client sends each year and their typical project value. A client who refers two ₹20,000 projects a year for three years adds ₹1,20,000 in value — often more than their own projects.
Should I give discounts to long-term clients?
A small discount for guaranteed volume or a retainer can make sense when the lifetime value is high. Avoid permanent discounts on one-off work — they reduce your effective rate without securing future income.
How do I track the value of each client?
Keep a simple spreadsheet of each client's projects, amounts, payment dates and referrals. Review it every few months with this calculator to see where your income really comes from.
What is a good client lifetime value?
There's no single number — it depends on your rates. A useful check is whether a client's yearly value covers a meaningful share of your income goal. Use our Freelance Rate Calculator to set that goal first.
💡 Pro Tip: Retaining an existing client costs 5-7x less than acquiring a new one. Before spending time on new client outreach, ask yourself: have I maximised the potential of every existing client relationship?
