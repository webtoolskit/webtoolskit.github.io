What is a SIP Calculator with Graph?

A SIP calculator — or Systematic Investment Plan calculator — helps you estimate the future value of your monthly mutual fund investments using compound interest. Our SIP calculator with graph goes further by showing you a visual chart of how your wealth grows year by year, making it easy to see the power of compounding visually.

This free SIP money calculator uses the standard SIP future value formula: FV = P × [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1] / r × (1 + r), where P is the monthly investment, r is the monthly return rate, and n is the total number of months.

Whether you're planning monthly SIPs in equity funds, debt funds, or hybrid funds — this calculator gives you an instant picture of your long-term wealth creation potential.

Why SIP in Mutual Funds Works

  • Rupee cost averaging — you buy more units when markets fall, fewer when they rise
  • Power of compounding — your returns start earning returns over time
  • Disciplined investing — removes emotional decision-making from investing
  • Flexible — start with as little as ₹500/month, increase anytime
  • No timing the market needed — consistent monthly investment beats lump sum timing
  • Tax efficient — ELSS SIPs qualify for ₹1.5 lakh deduction under Section 80C

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a SIP calculator with graph?
A SIP calculator with graph shows not just the final corpus but a year-by-year visual chart of how your invested amount and total value grow over time. The graph makes it easy to see exactly when compounding starts accelerating your wealth — typically after year 7–10 of consistent investing.
How does this SIP money calculator work?
This SIP money calculator uses the standard future value formula for systematic investments: FV = P × [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1] / r × (1 + r), where P = monthly SIP amount, r = monthly return rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n = total months. The result shows your estimated corpus at the end of your investment period.
What is a good monthly SIP amount?
There's no single right answer — it depends on your income and goals. A common rule is to invest at least 20% of your monthly income in SIPs. For wealth creation, even ₹5,000/month started early (age 25) at 12% returns grows to over ₹49 lakh in 20 years and over ₹1.76 crore in 35 years.
What is the SIP calculator 50 percent rule?
The 50% rule in SIP context refers to stepping up your SIP by 50% every few years as your income grows. For example, starting a ₹5,000 SIP and increasing it to ₹7,500 after 3 years significantly boosts your final corpus compared to a flat SIP — often by 40–60% more wealth at the end.
What is CAGR in SIP and how to calculate it?
CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) in SIP context refers to the annualised return your mutual fund has generated. For a monthly SIP calculator with CAGR, use the expected CAGR of your fund as the annual return rate input. Equity large-cap funds average 10–12% CAGR; mid-cap and small-cap funds average 12–16% over long periods.
What return rate should I use in the SIP mutual fund calculator?
Use 10–12% for conservative estimates with large-cap equity funds, 12–15% for mid-cap funds, and 8–9% for debt or hybrid funds. Never assume past returns guarantee future performance — use conservative estimates for financial planning.
Is SIP in mutual funds safe?
SIP in equity mutual funds carries market risk — returns are not guaranteed. However, long-term SIP investments of 7+ years in diversified equity funds have consistently created significant wealth historically. Debt fund SIPs are lower risk but also give lower returns (6–9%).
What is the minimum monthly SIP amount?
Most mutual funds allow SIP starting from ₹500 per month. Some funds like direct plans allow as low as ₹100/month. There is no maximum limit on SIP investment.
💡 Pro Tip: Start your SIP as early as possible. A ₹5,000 monthly SIP started at age 25 at 12% returns grows to over ₹1.76 crore by age 55 — nearly 3x more than starting at age 35 (₹49.5 lakh). Time in the market beats timing the market every single time.
