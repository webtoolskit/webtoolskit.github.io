Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is UTC?
A: UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) is the global time standard. All time zones are defined as offsets from UTC. IST (India Standard Time) is UTC+5:30, meaning India is 5 hours and 30 minutes ahead of UTC.
Q: Why does India have a 30-minute offset?
A: India chose UTC+5:30 in 1905 to create a single time zone for the entire country, placing it at the midpoint of its geographic spread. This is why India has an unusual 30-minute offset rather than a whole-hour one.
Q: What is Daylight Saving Time?
A: Many Western countries adjust their clocks forward by 1 hour in summer (Daylight Saving Time) to make better use of evening daylight. India, China, Japan, and many Asian countries do not observe DST.
Q: What is the best time for India-US meetings?
A: For India (IST) and US East Coast (EST): the overlap window is 6:30 PM–8:30 PM IST = 8:00–10:00 AM EST. Earlier than this and it's the middle of the night in India; later and the US workday is over.
💡 Pro Tip: When scheduling international meetings, always share the meeting time in both time zones explicitly (e.g., '3 PM IST / 9:30 AM UTC') to prevent confusion. Calendar apps like Google Calendar automatically convert to each attendee's local time zone.
