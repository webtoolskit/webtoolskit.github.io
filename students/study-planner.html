NEET Study Tips — Based on Toppers' Strategies

Physics (Most students' weak subject)

  • Focus on Mechanics, Electrostatics and Modern Physics — these carry the most weight
  • Solve at least 30 numerical problems per day — NEET Physics is 80% numericals
  • Use NCERT as base, then move to DC Pandey or HC Verma for practice

Chemistry (Highest scoring if done right)

  • Organic Chemistry reactions must be memorised — make a separate reactions notebook
  • Physical Chemistry needs daily numerical practice like Physics
  • Inorganic Chemistry — NCERT is everything, read it 5 times minimum

Biology (Highest weightage in NEET — 360 marks)

  • NCERT Biology (Class 11 + 12) covers 90%+ of NEET Biology questions
  • Read NCERT line by line — examiners pick exact sentences
  • Make flowcharts for chapters like Human Physiology and Plant Kingdom

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a tool that creates personalized NEET study schedules based on my weak subjects?
Yes — this is exactly what this free NEET study planner does. Select NEET as your exam type, mark your weakest subjects (Physics, Chemistry or Biology) as High Priority, and the tool generates a personalized daily study schedule that gives more hours to your weak subjects automatically. No signup required.
How many hours should a NEET aspirant study per day?
Most NEET toppers study 8–12 hours per day in the final 3–6 months. However, quality matters more than quantity. 6 focused hours with active recall and practice problems beats 12 distracted hours. Enter your realistic daily hours in the planner — it will build your schedule accordingly.
How should I divide study hours between Physics, Chemistry and Biology for NEET?
A common effective split is: Biology 40%, Chemistry 35%, Physics 25% — since Biology carries 360 out of 720 marks in NEET. However, if Physics is your weakest subject, increase Physics hours significantly. This planner lets you set your own priority for each subject based on your personal weak areas.
How many months of preparation does NEET require?
Most NEET toppers recommend 12–18 months of dedicated preparation for a first attempt. For droppers retaking NEET, 6–8 focused months of revision and test practice is typically sufficient. Use this study planner with your specific exam date to see how many weeks you have and plan accordingly.
Does this planner work for JEE preparation too?
Yes — select JEE from the exam type dropdown and Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics will be pre-loaded. The same weak subject prioritization logic applies. Mark your weakest JEE subject as High Priority and the planner allocates extra hours to it automatically.
How do I create a study timetable for weak subjects?
The key is to study your weakest subject when your energy is highest — typically in the morning for most students. Schedule your weak subject first, then move to average subjects, leaving your strongest subject for the end of the day. This planner marks weak subjects as High Priority and shows you exactly how many hours to dedicate daily.
💡 Pro Tip for NEET aspirants: Don't skip Biology revision even if it feels easy — NEET Biology questions come directly from NCERT lines. Read NCERT Biology (Class 11 + 12) at least 5 times. Use this study planner to set a dedicated Biology revision slot every single day, even during the final week before NEET.
