Frequently Asked Questions

Are these exchange rates live?
They are daily reference rates, not live trading rates. The converter loads mid-market rates from ExchangeRate-API once a day and shows the update date under the result. If the rates can't load, it uses the dated backup rates shown instead. Your bank, card or money-transfer service will use its own rate, so treat the result as a close estimate.
Why is my bank's rate different?
Banks and exchange counters add a margin to the mid-market rate — often 1–3%, sometimes more — and may charge a separate fee. Some money-transfer services charge a smaller margin plus a visible fee. Compare the total amount the other person receives, not just the headline rate.
What is the best way to convert currency?
For travel, compare a forex card, a card with a low foreign-currency markup and your bank's rate. For sending money, compare banks and licensed money-transfer services on both rate and fees. Airport counters are usually the most expensive option.
What is INR?
INR is the ISO code for the Indian Rupee, India's official currency, written with the symbol ₹. One rupee is divided into 100 paise.
How do I convert US dollars to Indian rupees?
Choose USD in the From box and INR in the To box, then enter the amount. The converter multiplies by the day's reference rate — for example, if 1 USD = ₹95.8, then $100 = ₹9,580. Tap ⇄ Swap to convert rupees to dollars instead.
How much is 1 UAE dirham in Indian rupees?
Select AED → INR above to see today's reference rate; the line under the result shows it as "1 AED = … INR". On 24 September 2026 the reference rate was about ₹26.1 per dirham. The dirham is pegged to the US dollar, so AED–INR moves almost exactly with USD–INR.
Which currencies can I convert here?
22 currencies: Indian Rupee, US Dollar, Euro, British Pound, UAE Dirham, Saudi Riyal, Qatari Riyal, Kuwaiti Dinar, Omani Rial, Singapore Dollar, Australian Dollar, Canadian Dollar, New Zealand Dollar, Japanese Yen, Chinese Yuan, Hong Kong Dollar, Swiss Franc, Thai Baht, Malaysian Ringgit, Nepalese Rupee, Bangladeshi Taka and Sri Lankan Rupee. You can convert between any two of them, not just to and from INR.
What is the mid-market exchange rate?
The mid-market rate is the midpoint between the buying and selling prices of a currency on global markets. It's the fairest reference rate, but it isn't what you'll usually get from a bank or exchange counter, which add a margin. This converter shows reference mid-market rates.
Why does the rupee's exchange rate change every day?
Exchange rates move with supply and demand for each currency — driven by trade, foreign investment flows, interest rates, oil prices and global events. The Reserve Bank of India can step in to smooth sharp moves, but it doesn't fix the rupee's value.
How much do cards charge for foreign currency payments?
Many Indian debit and credit cards add a foreign currency markup — commonly around 2–3.5% of the amount — plus GST on that fee. Some travel and premium cards have lower or zero markup. Check your card's schedule of charges before you travel or shop on foreign websites.
Is it better to exchange money in India or abroad?
It depends on the currency. For widely traded currencies such as USD, EUR, GBP or AED, you'll often get a fair rate in India from a forex card or authorised dealer. For less common currencies, exchanging a small amount in India and the rest on arrival can work better. Avoid airport counters where possible.
What is the best time to convert currency?
No one can reliably predict exchange rates. If you have a large or regular payment, converting in parts over a few weeks spreads the risk of a bad day. Use this converter to track the rate and compare offers from your bank and transfer services.
💡 Pro Tip: Airport exchange counters usually offer some of the poorest rates. Compare a forex card, a card with a low foreign-currency markup and your bank's rate before you travel — and check the total cost including fees, not just the rate.
