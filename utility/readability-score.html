What is this Tool?

The Readability Score Analyzer calculates your content's Flesch-Kincaid Reading Ease score — a widely used formula that measures how easy a piece of text is to read. The score is based on average sentence length and average word length. A higher score means easier to read. This tool also provides specific suggestions for improving your writing's clarity and accessibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Flesch-Kincaid grade level?
The Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level converts readability into a US school grade equivalent. A grade level of 8 means an 8th grader can understand the text. Grade 5–6 = very easy, Grade 7–8 = standard (ideal for most web content), Grade 9–12 = fairly difficult, Grade 13+ = college level. Most popular news websites target a grade level of 7–8.
How do I increase my Flesch-Kincaid grade level?
To increase your Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level (make text more advanced): use longer sentences, add multi-syllable vocabulary and technical terms. To decrease it (make text easier): shorten sentences to under 20 words, replace complex words with simpler alternatives, use active voice, and break long paragraphs into shorter ones. For most web content, a grade level of 7–9 is ideal.
What is a good Flesch Reading Ease score?
For general web content: aim for 60–70 (readable by most adults). For popular magazines: 60–70. For academic papers: 20–40. For legal documents: 30–50. The higher the score, the easier the text. UK government guidelines require a minimum score of 60 for public-facing content.
What is a good Flesch Reading Ease score for a website?
For most websites, a Flesch Reading Ease score of 60–70 is ideal — accessible to the average adult. For a general blog or news site, target 65–75. For an academic or professional site, 40–60 is acceptable. Scores below 50 will lose most general readers.
What is the Flesch-Kincaid formula?
The Flesch Reading Ease formula is: 206.835 − (1.015 × average sentence length) − (84.6 × average syllables per word). The Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level formula is: (0.39 × average sentence length) + (11.8 × average syllables per word) − 15.59. This tool applies both formulas automatically to any text you paste.
What does a Flesch-Kincaid score mean?
A Flesch-Kincaid score between 60–70 means your text is written in plain English, readable by most adults. Below 50 means the text is difficult — consider shortening sentences and replacing complex words. Above 70 means the text is easy and accessible. Academic papers typically score 20–40, legal documents 30–50, and tabloid newspapers 80–90.
What is the difference between Flesch Reading Ease and Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level?
Both use the same inputs (sentence length and syllables per word) but produce different outputs. Reading Ease gives a score where higher = easier (0–100). Grade Level gives a US school grade where lower = easier. They move in opposite directions — a Reading Ease of 70 corresponds roughly to a Grade Level of 7.
What is the Flesch reading ease score in the UK?
In the UK, the Flesch Reading Ease score is used by government departments, NHS writers, legal teams and content professionals to ensure plain English. The UK government and Plain English Campaign both recommend a minimum score of 60. Many legal documents score 30–50, which is why they are notoriously difficult for most people to understand.
What is Flesch reading ease in Word?
Microsoft Word has a built-in Flesch Reading Ease checker. Go to File → Options → Proofing → tick "Show readability statistics" → run Spell Check (F7) → scores appear at the end. The scores are identical to this online tool but Word requires running spell check first, which can be slower for long documents.
How do I improve my readability score?
To improve your Flesch-Kincaid score: (1) Shorten sentences — aim for under 20 words. (2) Use simpler words — replace multi-syllable words with shorter alternatives. (3) Use active voice. (4) Break long paragraphs into shorter ones. (5) Avoid jargon unless writing for specialists.
How does readability affect SEO?
Readability affects SEO indirectly through user engagement. Easy-to-read content reduces bounce rate and increases time on page — both signals Google uses to rank content. The Yoast SEO plugin flags Flesch-Kincaid scores below 60 as a readability issue. Clear writing also increases the chance of earning featured snippets.

Grade 7–8 is considered plain English suitable for most adults. Grade 12+ is college level. Grade 16+ is graduate level — typical of academic journals and legal documents.

How do I use Flesch-Kincaid in Word?
To use Flesch-Kincaid in Microsoft Word: (1) Click File → Options → Proofing. (2) Tick "Show readability statistics" under "When correcting spelling and grammar in Word". (3) Click OK. (4) Run spell check (F7 or Review → Spelling & Grammar). (5) After spell check completes, a readability statistics box appears showing your Flesch Reading Ease and Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level scores.
Should all content have a high readability score?
Not always. Academic, legal, or technical content necessarily uses complex terminology. The key is matching readability to your audience — don't simplify content that serves expert readers. A medical journal targeting doctors can score 20–30; a health blog targeting the public should score 60–70.
