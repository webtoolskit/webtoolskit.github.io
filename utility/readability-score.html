What is this Tool?

The Readability Score Analyzer calculates your content's Flesch-Kincaid Reading Ease score — a widely used formula that measures how easy a piece of text is to read. The score is based on average sentence length and average word length. A higher score means easier to read. This tool also provides specific suggestions for improving your writing's clarity and accessibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is a good Flesch Reading Ease score?
A: For general web content: aim for 60-70 (readable by 13-15 year olds). For popular magazines: 60-70. For academic papers: 20-40. For legal documents: 30-50. The higher the score, the easier the text.
Q: What makes text difficult to read?
A: Long sentences (20+ words), long words (3+ syllables), passive voice, complex vocabulary, and lack of clear structure all reduce readability. Short sentences and simple words dramatically improve accessibility.
Q: Does readability affect SEO?
A: Yes. Google's algorithm considers user engagement metrics like bounce rate and time on page. Content that's easier to read tends to keep readers engaged longer, reducing bounce rate and improving SEO rankings.
Q: Should all content have a high readability score?
A: Not always. Academic, legal, or technical content necessarily uses complex terminology. The key is matching readability to your audience — don't dumb down content that serves expert readers.
💡 Pro Tip: After writing any long piece, use this tool and then fix your three longest sentences by splitting them in two. This single action almost always improves both the readability score and the clarity of your writing significantly.
