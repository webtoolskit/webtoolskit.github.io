Frequently Asked Questions

What is the passing percentage in India?
Most Indian universities and boards require a minimum of 35% to pass in each subject and 40-45% aggregate. Professional courses like engineering and medicine often require 45-50% to pass.
How does CBSE grading work?
CBSE uses a 9-point scale: A1 (91-100%) = 10, A2 (81-90%) = 9, B1 (71-80%) = 8, B2 (61-70%) = 7, C1 (51-60%) = 6, C2 (41-50%) = 5, D (33-40%) = 4, E1/E2 (below 33%) = Fail.
What is a distinction in Indian universities?
A distinction is typically awarded for scoring 75% or above in aggregate. First Class is 60-74%, Second Class is 50-59%, and Pass is 35-49%. This varies by university.
What is B- as a percentage?
A B- grade corresponds to approximately 80–82% in most grading systems. In a 4.0 GPA scale, B- = 2.7 GPA. In percentage terms: A+ = 97–100%, A = 93–96%, A- = 90–92%, B+ = 87–89%, B = 83–86%, B- = 80–82%, C+ = 77–79%, C = 73–76%. Use the grade converter above for your specific institution's grading scale.
What is the pass mark for 200?
For a paper with maximum marks of 200, the pass mark at 35% is 70, at 40% is 80, and at 50% is 100. Most Indian universities require 35–40% to pass. Enter your total marks and passing percentage in the calculator above to find the exact pass mark for any maximum marks.
How do I calculate grade from percentage?
Grade from percentage: 90–100% = A+/O (Outstanding), 80–89% = A (Excellent), 70–79% = B+ (Very Good), 60–69% = B (Good), 50–59% = C (Average), 40–49% = D (Pass), below 40% = F (Fail). Different universities use slightly different cutoffs — check your institution's grading policy.
How do I calculate passing marks percentage?
Passing marks = (Pass percentage ÷ 100) × Maximum marks. For example, if max marks is 100 and pass percentage is 35%, passing marks = 35. If max marks is 75 and pass percentage is 40%, passing marks = 30. Use the calculator above — enter your maximum marks and the pass percentage to get the exact passing marks required.
What is 71/80 as a percentage?
71 out of 80 as a percentage = (71 ÷ 80) × 100 = 88.75%. This is a B+ or A- grade in most grading systems. Use the marks calculator above — enter 71 as marks obtained and 80 as maximum marks for instant percentage and grade.
Can I improve my percentage after getting results?
Yes, through revaluation (re-checking of answer sheets) or re-examination (supplementary exams). Many universities allow students to improve scores through back paper exams.

Grades, Percentages & Marks — Common Questions

What is B grade in percentage?
A B grade typically corresponds to 60–69% in most Indian universities using percentage-based grading. In a 10-point GPA system, B grade = 6.0–6.9 GPA. In CBSE: B1 = 71–80%, B2 = 61–70%. The exact percentage range for a B grade varies by institution — check your university's grading policy.
What is B+ grade in percentage?
A B+ grade typically corresponds to 65–74% in most Indian university systems. In a 10-point scale, B+ = 7.0 grade point (60–69%). In CBSE, there is no B+ — grades go from B1 (71–80%) to B2 (61–70%). Use the calculator above to find your exact percentage and see which grade you fall into.
What is 40/45 as a percentage?
40 out of 45 as a percentage = (40 ÷ 45) × 100 = 88.89%. This is an A or A+ grade. Enter your marks and maximum marks above for any combination — the calculator works for all marks and total marks values.
What is a second class percentage in India?
Second Class in Indian universities is typically 50–59% aggregate. First Class is 60–74%, Distinction is 75% and above, and Pass is 35–49%. Some universities use 45–59% as Second Class. Always check your specific university's classification cutoffs.
What percentage is first class?
First Class in most Indian universities is 60% and above (up to 74%). Some universities set the First Class cutoff at 55%. Distinction is typically awarded for 75% and above. Enter your marks above to see which classification you have achieved based on percentage-based grading.
What is a pass class percentage?
Pass Class in Indian universities is typically 35–49%. Some professional courses like engineering and medicine require 40–45% minimum to pass. The calculator shows ✅ Pass or ❌ Fail automatically based on the selected grading system.
How do I convert score to percentage?
Score to percentage formula: Percentage = (Marks Obtained ÷ Maximum Marks) × 100. For example, 450 out of 500 = (450 ÷ 500) × 100 = 90%. Enter your marks in the calculator above — it converts marks to percentage, grade and grade point instantly.
What is grading according to percentage in India?
Standard grading by percentage in India: O/A+ (90–100%) = Outstanding, A (75–89%) = Excellent/Distinction, B+/First Class (60–74%), B/Second Class (50–59%), C/Pass (35–49%), F (below 35%) = Fail. CBSE and university 10-point systems use slightly different cutoffs — select your system in the calculator above.
How do I calculate net marks?
Net marks in competitive exams with negative marking = (Correct answers × marks per question) − (Wrong answers × negative marks). For example, in NEET: 4 marks for correct, −1 for wrong. If you attempt 120 questions, get 100 right and 20 wrong: Net = (100 × 4) − (20 × 1) = 400 − 20 = 380.
How do I calculate passing marks for any exam?
Passing marks = (Pass percentage ÷ 100) × Maximum marks. At 35% for 200 marks = 70. At 40% for 100 marks = 40. At 50% for 75 marks = 37.5, round up to 38. Enter any combination of marks and pass percentage in the calculator above for the exact result.
What is 61/70 as a percentage?
61 ÷ 70 × 100 = 87.14%. Enter any marks and maximum marks above to convert them instantly.
What is 45 out of 50 as a percentage?
45 ÷ 50 × 100 = 90%.
What is 35 out of 50 as a percentage?
35 ÷ 50 × 100 = 70%.
How do I calculate the percentage for all subjects together?
Add the marks you scored in every subject, add the maximum marks for every subject, then divide and multiply by 100. For example, 412 out of 500 is 412 ÷ 500 × 100 = 82.4%.
Should internal marks be included in my percentage?
Use whatever your board or university includes on your marksheet. Most Indian universities add internal and external marks together for each subject, so include both unless your marksheet shows them separately.
